01 // The Operational Dilemma

The Misallocation of Human Call Capacity

Consumer lenders and collection agencies face a fundamental mathematical bottleneck: outbound calling capacity is strictly finite, while delinquent accounts number in the tens of thousands. In our benchmark dataset of 40,000 real-world overdue records, traditional collection desks followed two deeply flawed routines:

1. Calling High-Propensity Debtors First: Debtors with 85%+ recovery probability are frequently called by expensive human collectors, even though automated SMS or digital self-cure links would have achieved the exact same outcome for 0.05% of the operational cost.
2. Wasting Hours on Hopeless Default: High-balance accounts with near-zero repayment likelihood consume 40% of calling hours, yielding zero return while viable medium-risk accounts roll past the 90-day point of no return.

03 // Applied Mathematics • Probability Calibration

Why Uncalibrated Models Fail Debt Recovery

Modern gradient boosting frameworks (XGBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM) output raw pseudo-probabilities that are heavily distorted by imbalanced loss functions. An uncalibrated score of 0.65 rarely corresponds to a true 65% chance of debt collection.

LOD Engine applies Platt Scaling (Sigmoidal Post-Hoc Calibration) over an out-of-fold validation set, aligning raw model logits with empirical frequency:

04 // Retrospective • Lessons Learned

Principles for Applied Decision Science

1. Optimize for Business Payoff, Not Abstract Accuracy: A model with 92% classification accuracy that prioritizes easy-to-cure accounts produces virtually zero financial uplift. Modeling Expected Liquidation Value directly translates predictive AI into measurable banking cashflows.

2. Probability Calibration is Non-Negotiable: Decision thresholds derived from uncalibrated probabilities create distorted rankings in the middle probability bands (0.3 – 0.7), which is precisely where all operational profit is made or lost.
