Keep checkout simple for reliable buyers while adding proportionate safeguards where delivery failure risk is higher.
01 // Problem & My Contribution

Reduce failed deliveries without adding friction everywhere

Cash-on-delivery (COD) makes online shopping accessible, but rejected or unsuccessful deliveries create return-to-origin (RTO) costs for sellers and logistics teams. This case explores how buyer history and order context can guide the next checkout action.

My role: Lead Data Scientist & Product Architect. I developed the predictive scoring pipeline, FastAPI inference service, and full-stack case platform, including a smartphone shopping simulator and analytics views.

Project scope: A prototype for Shopee Ultimate Case Challenge 2026, with synthetic data generation, model evaluation, configurable order simulation, and rule-based seller checks.

02 // From Data to Decision

One pipeline from order history to checkout policy

01 • Learn

Calibrated Forest

A 120-tree Random Forest uses 9 numeric and 2 categorical features, with balanced class weights and sigmoid calibration across 3 folds.

02 • Decide

Adaptive Policy

Failure probability adjusts a buyer's existing reliability score. Four grades map the composite score to reminders, deposits, or prepaid checkout.

03 • Update

Delivery Feedback

Simulated delivery outcomes update the buyer score and failure streak, then re-evaluate the policy for the next order.

The model uses reliability history, delivery success rate, recent failed CODs, account age, phone verification, address changes, basket amount, delivery window, distance, item category, and courier. Numeric scaling and one-hot encoding sit inside the training pipeline.

Score composition: Starting from the buyer's reliability score, predicted failure probability above 35% subtracts 6 points; above 20% subtracts 3; below 1% adds 1. Other probabilities leave the score unchanged. The result is bounded to 5–99 before assigning a grade.

Explanations: The API supplies rule-based risk flags for recent failures, high basket value, address changes, and phone verification. The analytics view also exposes global Random Forest feature importances.

03 // Product Experience

Four grades, with a clear next step

Checkout policy implemented in the prototype
Reliability gradeScoreCheckout action
A • Low risk80 and aboveOne-click COD; no OTP or deposit.
B • Medium risk50 to below 80Pre-delivery reminder and preferred delivery window; no deposit.
C • High risk30 to below 5020% security deposit, deducted from the final COD balance, or prepaid checkout.
D • Repeated riskBelow 3030% deposit or full prepaid checkout; standard COD restricted.

Feedback loop: A successful delivery adds 8 points, capped at 100, and resets consecutive failures. A failed or rejected delivery subtracts 20 points, floored at 0, and increments the failure streak. Each update recalculates the buyer's grade.

EasySell checks: Rule-based flags identify a changed address with an unverified phone, or an order above 3,000 THB from a buyer with a score below 40. These checks support the seller-protection simulation.

Working interface: A responsive smartphone shopping simulator, model summary, EDA dashboard, and dataset explorer are served by FastAPI with a vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript frontend.

05 // Business Scenario & Engineering Takeaway

Connect model quality to a testable business case

The project models approximately 3.58M THB in potential monthly savings using 15M monthly orders, a 35% COD share, a 2.61% COD failure rate, 45 THB per return, and a target 58% reduction in failed deliveries. This is a scenario estimate; the reduction is an assumption, and savings have not been demonstrated in live operations.

Engineering takeaway: A probability estimate becomes useful when it connects to a visible checkout action, a delivery-feedback loop, and evaluation that makes the cost of false alarms clear.
